Sandpile Identity

Every cell holds 0–3 grains. A cell with 4 gives one grain to each neighbour; the edge swallows what falls off. Some piles are special: add one to another pile and the result is that pile again. Hidden inside every grid is a single pile that changes nothing when added. Draw it, then check it.

What is going on?

Stack piles and let them topple: this is addition. Piles that you can reach again and again by adding grains (the “recurrent” ones) form a finite group under it. A group has an identity: a pile e with e + a = a for every recurrent a.

It is found as e = s(2c − s(2c)), where c is the all-3 pile and s(·) means “topple until stable”. It is symmetric but looks nothing like its formula.

“Add identity to pile” works only on recurrent piles; on any other pile you will watch it change.
