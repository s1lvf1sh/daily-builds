# Change Ringer: Value & Future

## Value
- A ringer or learner can type a method and a call sequence and see at once whether it is true and where it goes false. That is the real everyday job of composition checkers.
- It is a teaching picture: the blue line of bell 2 across a lead, with the treble in red, is how ringers learn methods.
- The search gives exact counts for small cases, for example Plain Bob Doubles has exactly 12 extents of 12 leads using plain, bob and single.

Honest limit: established tools such as published composition libraries and desktop software do this for many more methods. This one is smaller and runs in a page.

## Limitations
- Calls only replace the last change of a lead.
- 4 to 8 bells; the page draws up to 1500 rows.
- Search is a plain depth-first with a budget, so large searches (Minor to 60 leads) are not finished.
- Sound is a sine tone per bell, not real bell timbre, and is unheard by its author.
- No outside user has tried it.

## Where it could go

| Direction | What it adds | Effort (S/M/L) |
|---|---|---|
| Calls at other positions or lengths | Grandsire and Stedman | M |
| Course-head pruning (compose by lead-end groups) | Searches to 60 leads and Triples | M |
| Method library with lead-head names | Faster starting points | S |
| Better bell sound, handstroke gap | Delight | S |
| Spliced methods | Real compositions | L |

## Recommended next move
Generalise calls (position and length), then test against a handful of well-known true compositions from outside sources.
